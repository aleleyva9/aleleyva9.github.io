Alejandra Leyva

Political scientist and AI governance researcher. She holds an MSc in Security, Peacebuilding and Diplomacy from Loughborough University London and has previously worked at Mexico's highest electoral court and Meta. Her current research focuses on international AI coordination, the role of middle powers, and post-control scenarios for advanced AI.

LinkedIn Substack

Areas of inquiry

Middle Powers and Institutional Preparedness

  • How countries outside the frontier can combine complementary sources of leverage over frontier AI developers into credible safeguards
  • Mapping how national institutions recognise and respond to AI risks, with a risk map of Mexico's institutional agenda on AI
  • Exposure to foreign AI systems and the bargaining position of states in the AI supply chain

Post-Control Scenarios

  • What gives humans claims, standing and bargaining power once advanced AI can no longer be fully controlled
  • How governments and organisations can prepare for AI systems that become difficult to control
  • Convening expert roundtables across governance, economics, alignment and artificial minds

Autonomous Weapons

  • Preventing AI systems from autonomously targeting people, and addressing risks to human rights, human agency and accountability
  • AI-enabled autonomous weapons in civilian and law-enforcement contexts
  • Coalition building and advocacy campaigns for state-level policy

Paper2026

AI Risks and Sovereignty. What Mexico's Institutional Agenda Recognises and Addresses

Apart Research, Global South AI Safety Hackathon · published on Zenodo in English and Spanish

I developed a risk map covering seven AI risk scenarios and their pathways, and built an original dataset of Mexican government actions on AI to examine which risks institutions recognise and where policy gaps remain. The analysis finds that existing measures largely address visible harms, while more systemic AI risks receive limited attention.

Read on Zenodo
Campaign2026

No Killer Robots

Sapiens First · California

A California-focused campaign advocating restrictions on AI-enabled weapons that autonomously select or target people, including their use in civilian and law-enforcement contexts. The proposals also call for human authorisation before engaging vehicles that may carry people, while distinguishing these cases from defensive systems targeting incoming missiles or other uncrewed objects.
